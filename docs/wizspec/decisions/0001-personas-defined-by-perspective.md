# Personas are defined by perspective, not personality

A persona is the durable, user-authored simulation of a stakeholder that skills in this repository
cast into subagents. The obvious reading of the word is a personality: a voice and a temperament. We
define it instead by perspective: the knowledge boundary, the stakes, and what a bad outcome costs.
The beginner student that motivated the design was useful because it was forbidden from using
knowledge a beginner would not have; strip that boundary and a model produces an expert with a
student's voice, reporting confusion it does not have and missing confusion it would. Voice stays in
the persona as flavour.

## Consequences

- The persona interview spends most of its questions on the knowledge boundary and the stakes, and
  one on voice.
- A persona is a person, not a job, so it is role-agnostic: the same persona can be cast as
  reviewer, writer, or respondent, and the consuming skill supplies the role per run.
- Personas saved in users' repositories follow this shape, so reversing the decision means
  migrating them.
