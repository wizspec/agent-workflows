# The child ignores difficulty, and the parent narrows by what can be done

The obvious brief asks for an idea that is realistic. We decided instead that the child imagines
without weighing how hard its idea would be to make, and that grounding narrows it afterwards. This
mirrors how ideas from people without technical knowledge get grounded by the people who will build
them. Agents drop ideas they judge hard before anyone has checked, and their sense of hard is
calibrated to how long a person would take. So the ideas that are lost are often the most
distinctive, and they are lost for a reason that may not even apply.

## Consequences

- The parent judges what is realistic by what can actually be done with the means available,
  including agents that work quickly and at scale. "Hard" is not an answer; what it would take is.
- A real cost the user might choose to pay is the user's decision. The child keeps that part, and
  the report sets the cost out rather than the parent or the child cutting it.
- The report shows the idea as first imagined beside the narrowed one, so the user can see and
  reverse each cut.
- Some rounds go to parts of an idea that turn out to be impossible. We accept that cost.
