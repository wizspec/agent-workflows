# The brief teaches the child no way of having ideas

The brief limits what reaches the child: no precedent, the idea before the questions, a heart before
any precedent, and no file access. It does not tell the child how to have the idea. An earlier draft
did, asking the child to name the obvious version and look elsewhere and suggesting ideas borrowed
from unrelated fields. Those were our own beliefs about creativity, handed over before the idea,
which is exactly what the skill forbids for every other kind of precedent. They would also have made
every idea come back in the same shape. Do not add creative techniques, steps, or pushes such as
"think big" to the brief; a permission, such as not ruling things out for difficulty, is fine.
