# The child gets knowledge only after it has its idea

The simplest fresh-eyes design keeps precedent from the child entirely and cuts it off from outside
sources. We decided instead that what matters is timing. Until the child commits to an idea and
names its heart, it has only the brief; after that, it may ask the parent for precedent and use the
web. Knowledge that arrives before the idea becomes its starting point, which is the repetition the
skill exists to avoid. Knowledge that arrives after is information the child can weigh against an
idea it already holds.

## Consequences

- The parent never volunteers precedent, but answers when the child asks, marks the answer as
  precedent, and leaves the child free to ignore it.
- The child may search the web. The brief explains why looking at examples of the thing itself
  before having an idea pulls towards what already exists, rather than forbidding it.
- A child that takes precedent on board can quietly drop its heart, so the parent checks once for
  drift and reports what the child said.
